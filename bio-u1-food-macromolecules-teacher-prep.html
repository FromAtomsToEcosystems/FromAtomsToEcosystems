Food Macromolecules Lab: Teacher Prep Guide

For Testing Foods for Macromolecules (bio-u1-food-macromolecules-lab.html). Students absent for the lab use the simulation (bio-u1-food-macromolecules-lab-sim.html), which needs no prep. Standard: NGSS HS-LS1-6 (carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from sugar combine with other elements to form amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules).

3. Controls and food samples

Each group needs about 10 mL of every sample (2 mL in each of three tube tests, plus a swab for the paper). Make about 150 mL of each per period, or 300 to 400 mL for a full day. Put each in a labeled cup or bottle with its own pipette. Label every cup "Lab use only: do not eat."

SampleHow to make it
Water (negative control)Tap or distilled water.
Glucose solution (Benedict's control)1 g glucose (dextrose) in 100 mL water (1%). Corn syrup or 2 crushed glucose tablets in 100 mL water also work.
Starch solution (iodine control)1 g soluble starch or cornstarch mixed into a little cold water, then stirred into 100 mL boiling water for 1 minute. Cool before use.
Egg white solution (Biuret control)1 egg white stirred gently into 100 mL water (do not whip). Make it fresh each day and keep it cold.

6. Safety

Biuret reagent is corrosive (copper sulfate in sodium hydroxide). It can burn skin and eyes. Goggles stay on the whole lab. If it touches skin, rinse with plenty of water for several minutes; for eyes, use the eyewash for 15 minutes and get help.
Food allergies: ask before the lab (peanut, milk, egg, wheat, soy). Peanut residue can cause a reaction by touch for some students. If anyone in the room has a peanut allergy, replace peanut with sunflower seeds (also protein and lipid) and keep all peanut products out of the room. A student with a food allergy can do the simulation instead.
  • Hot water bath: hot plates and hot water burn. Use test tube holders, point tube openings away from people, and never leave a hot plate on unattended. Let water cool before emptying.
  • Benedict's solution is an irritant and contains copper. Iodine stains skin and clothes and is harmful if swallowed. Gloves help with both.
  • No tasting. Nothing in the lab is eaten, even the foods. Label all cups "Lab use only."
  • Students wash hands before leaving.

7. Timing (about 50 minutes)

  1. Setup page and check questions: 10 min
  2. Predictions locked in: 5 min (the observation tables stay locked until predictions are locked)
  3. Bench work: about 20 min. Start the Benedict's tubes first: do the iodine and Biuret tests and rub the paper while the tubes heat and the paper dries.
  4. Record results, analysis, and CER: 15 to 20 min (finish CER for homework if needed)

8. Cleanup and waste

  • Pour used Benedict's and Biuret tubes (they contain copper) into a labeled waste beaker. Dispose of it following your district's chemical waste rules. Do not pour copper waste down the drain unless your district allows it.
  • Iodine tubes: small amounts can usually be rinsed down the drain with lots of water (check your district rules).
  • Food waste and paper go in the trash. Wash tubes with a test tube brush and soapy water; a little vinegar removes copper stains.
  • Turn off and unplug hot plates. Wipe tables with soapy water.

9. Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Glucose control stays blue in Benedict'sThe water bath is not hot enough or the tubes were not heated long enough. Use 80 to 100 °C water for a full 5 minutes.
Starch control is not blue-blackStarch was not boiled, or the iodine is old. Make fresh starch solution; replace iodine stored in light.
Water turns violet in Biuret or blue-black in iodineA shared pipette or dirty tube. Great discussion point: the page asks about it and grades against what the group recorded.
Every paper spot looks wetThe paper is not dry. Wait the full 10 minutes (or use a hair dryer on low) and hold it to a window.
Group can't decide between two colorsHold the tube against white paper next to the color key. Record the closest match; the analysis uses their recorded color.
