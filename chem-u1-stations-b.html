Unit 1 Stations B

Teacher guide: Unit 1 Stations B (radioactive decay, half-life, fission, and fusion)

Use after Day 8 (half-life lab). 8 stations, one per group of 4, about 10 minutes each. Students carry the paper reflection sheet or use chem-u1-stations-b-reflection.html on a Chromebook (it saves and sends scores to the Google Sheet).

  1. Print the station pages (one per table) and the card pages.
  2. Fold-over cards (Stations 2 and 6): cut out each card, fold on the dashed line so the answer hides behind the question, and tape the fold.
  3. Optional add-ons: a sheet of paper, aluminum foil, and a heavy book at Station 1 (to show the barriers), and a periodic table at Stations 2, 3, and 5.
  4. Rotation: 1 → 2 → … → 8 → 1, about 10 minutes each. Stations 3 and 5 (calculations) take the longest.

The answer key is behind the teacher code.

Reading · about 10 min

Directions

  1. Read the three opinions with your group.
  2. Answer the reflection questions.

Should Our Town Build a Nuclear Power Plant?

Maria (for): "A nuclear plant makes huge amounts of electricity day and night, and it releases almost no carbon dioxide while running. One small fuel pellet makes as much energy as a ton of coal."

Devon (against): "The used fuel stays radioactive for thousands of years, and we still don’t have a permanent place to store it. Accidents like Fukushima are rare, but they can force people to leave their homes."

Dr. Lee (scientist): "Fusion could someday give us clean energy with little long-lived waste, using fuel from seawater. But no one has built a fusion plant that makes more energy than it uses, yet."
