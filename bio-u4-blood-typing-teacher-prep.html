Blood Typing Lab: Teacher Prep Guide

For Blood Typing Lab: Crime Scene Investigation (bio-u4-blood-typing-lab.html). Students absent for the lab use the simulation (bio-u4-blood-typing-lab-sim.html), which needs no prep.

1. How the trick works

  • Both "serum" bottles (Anti-A serum and Anti-B serum) contain plain white vinegar.
  • Milk blood (whole milk + red food coloring) curdles when vinegar is added. It looks grainy, with dark red specks in lighter liquid. This models agglutination.
  • Starch blood (water + a little cornstarch + red food coloring) looks just as opaque but stays smooth in vinegar.
  • You set each sample's blood type by choosing which fake blood goes in its A test cup and its B test cup:

Water control: each group adds water (not serum) to a drop of the Crime scene A-test blood. Water never curdles milk, so the control always shows no clumping. That proves the serum causes the clumping. The control is most convincing when the crime scene A-test cup holds milk blood (Scenarios 1 and 3 below).

2. Recipes

Milk blood (clumps)

  • 3 cups (about 750 mL) whole milk
  • About 1 teaspoon (5 mL) red liquid food coloring, plus 1 to 2 drops of blue or green to darken it to a blood red
  • Stir. Room-temperature milk curdles faster than cold milk.

Starch blood (no clumping)

  • 3 cups (about 750 mL) water
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch (about 1 teaspoon per cup), mixed first into a little cold water so it doesn't lump
  • The same amount of food coloring as the milk blood
  • Pour a little of each side by side in white cups. They should look the same. If the starch blood looks see-through, add a pinch more cornstarch; if it is lighter, add a drop more coloring.
  • Cornstarch settles. Stir or shake it right before you fill cups, and swirl cups before each period.

Test before class

Put 3 drops of each blood in a white plate, add 3 to 5 drops of vinegar, and stir. Within 1 minute the milk blood should be grainy and the starch blood smooth. If the milk is slow: use more vinegar (5 drops), wait 2 minutes, or let the milk warm to room temperature.

4. Labeling

  • Cup labels (8 per group): Crime scene A test, Crime scene B test, Suspect 1 A test, Suspect 1 B test, Suspect 2 A test, Suspect 2 B test, Suspect 3 A test, Suspect 3 B test.
  • Bottle labels: Anti-A serum, Anti-B serum, Water. Real anti-A reagent is blue and anti-B is yellow, so blue and yellow labels look authentic. Keep both serum bottles identical otherwise.
  • Use the same cup type and the same fill level for every cup. Opaque or lidded cups hide any small difference between milk and starch blood.
  • Write the scenario number on the bottom of each group's bin or tray, never on the cups.
  • Tray layout (students see this on the lab page): rows Crime scene, Suspect 1, Suspect 2, Suspect 3; columns A test and B test; one control spot at the bottom.

Analysis questions 11 and 12 (Punnett square): Suspect 1 claims parents AB × O, who can only have type A or B children. In Scenarios 1 and 4 Suspect 1's story checks out (B, A); in Scenarios 2 and 3 it doesn't (AB), which makes a good discussion.

6. Safety

Milk allergy: ask before the lab. A student with a milk allergy should wear gloves and goggles and let partners handle the cups, or do the simulation instead. Soy milk also curdles in vinegar and can replace dairy milk, but check for soy allergies first.
  • No tasting. Even though the materials are food, nothing in a lab is eaten or tasted. Students are not told what the blood or serum is made of.
  • Goggles: vinegar stings if it gets in eyes. Rinse eyes with water if that happens.
  • Gloves: food coloring stains hands and clothes.
  • Wipe spills right away (milk gets slippery and sour). Everyone washes hands after.
  • Both serum bottles smell like vinegar. That's fine: they smell the same, so it gives nothing away. If asked, "real reagents have a smell too."

7. Timing (about 50 minutes)

  1. Setup page and check questions: 10 to 12 min
  2. Predictions locked in: 5 min (the observation table stays locked until predictions are locked)
  3. Bench work: about 10 min (3 drops per well, serum, stir, wait 1 minute), then record: 5 min
  4. Analysis and CER: 20 min

8. Cleanup

  • Rinse wells into the sink with plenty of water. Toss toothpicks and paper towels in the trash.
  • Wash trays and reusable droppers in warm soapy water so milk doesn't sour in them.
  • Keep milk blood refrigerated between periods; let it sit out about 10 minutes before use. Throw out milk blood at the end of the day and make it fresh each lab day. Starch blood keeps a few days in the fridge (stir before use).
  • Wipe tables with soapy water.

9. Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Milk blood doesn't clumpUse whole milk at room temperature; add 5 drops of vinegar; stir; wait 2 minutes.
Starch blood looks grainyThe cornstarch settled or clumped. Stir or shake before pouring; use the amount above.
Students can tell the bloods apartMatch color and opacity side by side; use opaque or lidded cups.
A control well clumpedSerum got into it (shared toothpick or dropper). Good teachable moment: the page asks about it, and accepts "something went wrong" if that is what the group recorded.
Group's results don't match the scenarioThe page grades analysis against what the group recorded, so their analysis is still gradable. Talk through it: which well was mixed up?
