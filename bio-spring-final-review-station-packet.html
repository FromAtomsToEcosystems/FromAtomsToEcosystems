Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 4 envelopes: label them 1, 3, 4, 7
  • Scissors
  • Scratch paper for Punnett squares (Station 2)

Station 2: Punnett Square Challenge

Each card gives a cross. Make a Punnett square on your sheet (or scratch paper), predict the answer, then flip to check.

Directions:

  1. Look at the 6 cards at this station. Do not open them yet.
  2. For each card, talk it over with your team and write your prediction.
  3. When you have predicted all of them, flip the cards open to check. Write the actual answer.
  4. Reset: fold every card closed.

Answer Key

Station 1: Big Ideas Match

1. Allele: One version of a gene (B (black fur) or b (yellow fur)); Codominance: Both alleles show fully (Type AB blood); Sex-linked trait: A trait whose gene is on the X chromosome (Red-green color blindness); Natural selection: Better-suited individuals survive and reproduce more (Tuskless elephants during heavy poaching); Speciation: A new species forms (Squirrels on two rims of the Grand Canyon); Carrying capacity: The largest population an area can support (A deer herd that levels off); Keystone species: Has a much bigger effect than its numbers (Sea otters in a kelp forest); Trophic level: A feeding step in a food chain (Primary consumer).

2. Answers vary. Example: alleles are what natural selection acts on; the allele for a helpful trait becomes more common.

Station 2: Punnett Square Challenge

1. 1: 1/4 (25%); 2: 50%; 3: 1 pink : 1 white; 4: 50%; 5: Type A or type B (50% each); 6: All speckled (black and white feathers).

2. Answers vary.

Station 3: From Mutation to Ecosystem

1. B C F E D A (A random mutation gives one cactus mouse an allele for darker fur; The mouse passes the allele to its pups through its sex cells; A lava flow cools into dark rock where the mice live; Owls spot light mice on the dark rock and eat more of them; Dark mice survive and reproduce more, so the dark allele becomes common; With fewer mice easy to catch, the owl population shrinks).

2. Unit 4: mutation, alleles, inheritance. Unit 5: natural selection by owls. Unit 6: predator and prey populations.

3. No. The mutation happened by chance; the dark rock only made it helpful.

Station 4: Which Type of Selection?

1. Directional: Lizards with bigger toe pads survive a hurricane, Bacteria become more resistant to an antibiotic each year, Tuskless elephants become common during poaching; Stabilizing: Very small and very large human babies survive less often, Goldenrod galls that are medium sized escape both wasps and birds, Birds that lay about 5 eggs raise the most chicks; Disruptive: Small and large male salmon father the most young, Seedcrackers with small or large beaks eat seeds better than medium beaks.

2. Directional: the peak shifts. Stabilizing: the curve gets taller and narrower. Disruptive: two peaks form.

Station 5: Wolves and Moose on Isle Royale

1. Moose went up (664 to about 1,200) because fewer predators ate them.

2. Predation (+/−): the wolf is helped, the moose is harmed.

3. Genetic drift (chance loss of alleles in a small population), plus inbreeding.

4. Gene flow.

Station 6: Ecosystem Change Cards

1. 1: Urchins increase and the kelp disappears; 2: The herd crashes; 3: Fish-eating birds get the highest levels; 4: Native ants and the lizards that eat them decline; 5: More CO₂ in the air; 6: Elk decrease and streamside willows grow back.

2. Answers vary. Example: kelp → urchin → otter; otter down, urchin up, kelp down.

Station 7: Which Unit Is It?

1. Unit 4: Genetics: A karyotype shows three copies of chromosome 21, Hemophilia shows up mostly in boys, IA and IB are codominant; Unit 5: Evolution: Whale flippers have the same bones as a human arm, Darwin's finches have different beaks on different islands, Fireflies with different flash patterns do not mate; Unit 6: Ecology: Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level, Barnacles riding on a whale, A population levels off at K.

2. Answers vary.

Station 8: Pesticide-Resistant Mosquitoes

1. The percent killed fell from 96% to 20% over 9 years.

2. A few mosquitoes already had a resistance allele. The spray killed the others, so resistant mosquitoes survived and reproduced and passed on the allele. Each year more were resistant.

3. 1/4. (This assumes R is dominant.)

4. They could decline from less food (or switch to other prey).
