Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 4 envelopes: label them 1, 2, 3, 5
  • Scissors

Answer Key

Station 1: Big Ideas Match

1. Macromolecule: Large molecule built from monomers (A protein made of amino acids); Homeostasis: Keeping the inside of the body stable (Sweating on a hot day); Enzyme: Protein that speeds up a reaction (Amylase breaks down starch); Osmosis: Diffusion of water across a membrane (A raisin swells in water); Photosynthesis: Light energy makes glucose (A leaf in sunlight); Cellular respiration: Glucose and oxygen make ATP (Your muscles while you run); Transcription: DNA to mRNA (TAC becomes AUG); Mutation: A change in DNA (Sickle cell hemoglobin).

2. Answers vary. Example: enzymes are proteins (macromolecules) built by translation.

Station 2: Which Organelle?

1. Nucleus: DNA is copied (replication), mRNA is made (transcription); Ribosome: Amino acids are linked (translation), tRNA anticodons pair with codons; Mitochondrion: The Krebs cycle runs, About 32 ATP made by the ETC; Chloroplast: Light splits water, The Calvin cycle builds glucose; Cell membrane: Osmosis and diffusion, The sodium-potassium pump works.

2. Chloroplasts (light energy to chemical energy in glucose) and mitochondria (glucose to ATP).

Station 3: From Gene to Lunch

1. B C F E D A (The amylase gene in a salivary gland cell is transcribed into mRNA; The mRNA leaves the nucleus and is translated at a ribosome; The chain of amino acids folds into amylase, an enzyme; In your mouth, amylase breaks starch from your bread into sugars by hydrolysis; Glucose is absorbed into your blood; insulin helps cells take it in; Your cells break down the glucose by cellular respiration to make ATP).

2. Unit 3: transcription and translation. Unit 1: the enzyme, hydrolysis, insulin (homeostasis). Unit 2: cellular respiration (and glucose crossing membranes).

3. Starch might not fit, so it would not be broken down as well.

Station 4: Mixed Review Cards

1. 1: 7; 2: They can denature; 3: Out of the cell; 4: Glucose and oxygen; 5: AUG CCA; 6: Lactic acid fermentation.

2. Answers vary.

Station 5: Does It Cost ATP?

1. Uses ATP: A muscle contracting, Active transport (low to high), Building a protein at a ribosome, Exocytosis of insulin, Copying DNA; No ATP needed: Oxygen diffusing into a cell, Water moving by osmosis, Glucose through a channel, high to low.

2. Cellular respiration (mostly in the mitochondria), using glucose and oxygen.

Station 6: Bread Dough and Temperature

1. About 35 °C. 2. Carbon dioxide, from alcoholic fermentation (yeast respiration).

3. The heat denatured the yeast's enzymes (and killed the yeast), so fermentation stopped.

4. Warm the bowl to about 35 °C; the dough would start to rise because cold only slows enzymes.

Station 7: From DNA to Disease

1. 1: One amino acid changes: sickle cell; 2: One amino acid missing: cystic fibrosis; 3: No change (silent); 4: Adults can digest milk; 5: Frameshift: protein is badly changed; 6: No insulin: blood sugar stays high.

2. Answers vary. Example: no insulin gene means no insulin protein, so the blood sugar feedback loop cannot lower glucose.

Station 8: The Marathon

1. Muscles need more oxygen and glucose for cellular respiration to make ATP, so the heart pumps faster.

2. Negative feedback.

3. Muscles are not getting enough oxygen, so they use lactic acid fermentation.

4. Saltier blood is hypertonic to her cells, so water moves out of the cells by osmosis (another reason to drink water).
