TermDefinitionExample
The Periodic Table
ElementA pure substance made of only one kind of atom.Oxygen (O), sodium (Na)
Atomic numberThe number of protons in an atom. It identifies the element.Carbon is 6
Group (family)A column of the periodic table. Elements in a group have similar properties.Group 1, group 17
PeriodA row of the periodic table.Period 2: Li to Ne
MetalShiny element that conducts heat and electricity; found on the left side.Iron, copper
NonmetalElement that is usually dull, brittle, and a poor conductor; found on the right side.Sulfur, chlorine
MetalloidElement along the staircase with properties of both metals and nonmetals.Silicon, boron
Alkali metalsGroup 1 metals. Very reactive; 1 valence electron.Sodium, potassium
Alkaline earth metalsGroup 2 metals. Reactive; 2 valence electrons.Magnesium, calcium
HalogensGroup 17 nonmetals. Very reactive; 7 valence electrons.Fluorine, chlorine
Noble gasesGroup 18. Unreactive because their outer shell is full.Neon, argon
Transition metalsThe metals in the middle block (groups 3–12).Iron, copper, gold
Bohr Models and Electrons
Bohr modelA drawing of an atom with electrons in circles (shells) around the nucleus.Na: 2, 8, 1
Energy level (shell)A ring where electrons are found. Holds 2, then 8, then 8 in the first three.Shells 1, 2, 3
Valence electronsElectrons in the outermost shell. They decide how an atom bonds.Cl has 7
Electron-dot diagramAn element symbol with dots for its valence electrons.Na with 1 dot
Flame testHeating a metal in a flame: electrons jump up, fall back, and give off a color of light.Copper burns blue-green
Ions
IonAn atom that has gained or lost electrons, so it has a charge.Na⁺, Cl⁻
CationA positive ion. Metals lose electrons to form cations.Mg²⁺
AnionA negative ion. Nonmetals gain electrons to form anions.O²⁻
Octet ruleAtoms gain, lose, or share electrons to get 8 in their outer shell.Na loses 1 → 8 left
ChargeProtons minus electrons. Losing electrons makes it positive; gaining makes it negative.Al³⁺ lost 3
Periodic Trends
Periodic trendA pattern in a property across a period or down a group.Atoms get bigger down a group
Atomic radiusThe size of an atom. Increases down a group, decreases across a period.K is bigger than Na
Ionization energyThe energy needed to remove an electron from an atom.Highest for noble gases
ElectronegativityHow strongly an atom pulls on shared electrons.Fluorine is highest
Nuclear chargeThe pull of the protons in the nucleus. More protons pull electrons closer.Across a period
ShieldingInner electrons block the outer electrons from the nucleus's pull.More shells, more shielding
ReactivityHow easily an element reacts. Alkali metals get more reactive down the group.K reacts faster than Li
Ionic Bonding
Ionic bondThe attraction between positive and negative ions after a metal gives electrons to a nonmetal.NaCl
Ionic compoundA compound made of ions; usually a metal + a nonmetal.MgO, CaCl₂
Electron transferElectrons moving from a metal atom to a nonmetal atom.Na gives 1 to Cl
Crystal latticeThe repeating 3D pattern of + and − ions in an ionic solid.Salt crystals
SubscriptA small number in a formula that tells how many of the atom before it.The 2 in CaCl₂
-ide endingThe ending for the nonmetal in an ionic name.chlorine → chloride
BrittleShatters when hit. Ionic crystals are brittle.Salt crystal
Covalent Bonding
Covalent bondA bond where two nonmetal atoms share a pair of electrons.H–H, Cl–Cl
MoleculeA group of atoms held together by covalent bonds.H₂O, CO₂
Shared pairTwo electrons, one from each atom, held between them. Drawn as a line.The line in Cl–Cl
Lone pairA pair of valence electrons on one atom that is not shared.Two on O in H₂O
Unpaired electronA valence electron by itself; it can pair with another atom's to form a bond.Cl has 1
Lewis structureA drawing of a molecule: lines for shared pairs, dots for lone pairs.H–O–H
Double bondTwo shared pairs (4 electrons) between the same two atoms.O=O
Triple bondThree shared pairs (6 electrons) between the same two atoms.N≡N
Metallic Bonding
Metallic bondThe attraction between positive metal ions and a sea of free electrons.Copper wire
Sea of electronsValence electrons that move freely through a whole metal.In any metal
DelocalizedNot stuck to one atom; free to move.Electrons in a metal
MalleableCan be hammered into thin sheets without breaking.Aluminum foil
DuctileCan be pulled into a wire.Copper wire
LusterA shiny surface that reflects light.Polished silver
ConductorA material that lets electricity or heat flow easily.Metals
AlloyA mixture of metals, held by metallic bonds.Brass (copper + zinc)
