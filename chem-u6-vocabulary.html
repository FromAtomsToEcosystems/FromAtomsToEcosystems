Heat and Calorimetry
Specific heatThe energy needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 °C (J/g·°C).Water: 4.18 J/g·°C
q = mcΔTHeat = mass × specific heat × change in temperature.50 g water, ΔT 10 °C → 2,090 J
ΔTChange in temperature: final − initial.20 → 30 °C: ΔT = 10 °C
CalorimeterAn insulated container used to measure heat flow.Foam cup
Heat flowEnergy moves from hot to cold until temperatures are equal.Hot metal → cool water
Energy in Reactions
ExothermicReleases energy; surroundings warm; ΔH is negative.Hand warmer
EndothermicAbsorbs energy; surroundings cool; ΔH is positive.Instant cold pack
Potential energy diagramA graph of stored energy as a reaction goes from reactants to products.The energy hill
Activation energy (Eₐ)Energy needed to start a reaction: reactants to the top of the hill.Striking a match
Activated complexThe high-energy arrangement at the top of the hill.Top of the hump
ΔH (enthalpy change)Energy of products − energy of reactants.50 − 150 = −100 kJ
Reaction Rates
Reaction rateHow fast reactants turn into products (amount ÷ time).40 mL in 10 s = 4.0 mL/s
Collision theoryParticles react only when they collide with enough energy and the right orientation.Most collisions bounce
ConcentrationHow much solute is packed into a volume; higher means more collisions.Pure O₂ vs. air
Surface areaExposed outside of a solid; smaller pieces have more.Powder vs. a cube
CatalystSpeeds up a reaction by lowering Eₐ; not used up.Enzymes
Equilibrium
Reversible reactionA reaction that goes both ways, written with ⇌.N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
EquilibriumForward and reverse rates are equal; amounts stay constant.A sealed soda
Le Chatelier's principleA system at equilibrium shifts to partly undo a stress.Add reactant → shift right
StressA change in concentration, temperature, or gas pressure.Squeezing a gas
Solutions
Solute / solventThe substance dissolved / the substance doing the dissolving.Salt / water
SolubilityThe most solute that dissolves in 100 g of water at a temperature.KNO₃: 64 g at 40 °C
Saturated / unsaturated / supersaturatedAt the limit / below it / above it (unstable).On / below / above the curve
Molarity (M)Moles of solute ÷ liters of solution.0.50 mol in 0.250 L = 2.0 M
DilutionAdding solvent to lower concentration; moles stay the same.M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
Stock solutionA concentrated solution kept on hand to dilute.2.00 M HCl
Acids and Bases
AcidReleases H⁺ in water; pH below 7; tastes sour.Lemon juice
BaseReleases OH⁻ in water; pH above 7; slippery.Soapy water
pH scale0–14; 7 is neutral; each step is 10 times.pH 3 is 100× more acidic than pH 5
IndicatorA substance that changes color with pH.Litmus, phenolphthalein
NeutralizationAcid + base → salt + water.HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
