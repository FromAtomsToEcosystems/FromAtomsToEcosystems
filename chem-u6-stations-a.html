Unit 6 Stations A

Teacher guide: Unit 6 Stations A (energy, rates, and equilibrium)

Use after Day 6 (Le Chatelier). 8 stations, one per group of 4, about 10 minutes each (80 minutes total). Students carry the reflection sheet (paper) or use chem-u6-stations-a-reflection.html on a Chromebook (it saves and sends scores to the Google Sheet).

  1. Print the station pages (8 pages, one per table) and the card pages (3 pages).
  2. Fold-over cards (Stations 3 and 7): cut out each card, fold on the dashed line so the answer hides behind the question, and tape or glue the fold. Students predict, then lift the flap.
  3. Cut cards (Stations 4 and 5): cut out and put each set in a labeled envelope. Remind groups to put the cards back in the envelope before rotating.
  4. Optional hands-on add-ons: a hand warmer and an instant cold pack at Station 3; glow sticks in cups of hot and ice water at Station 5. These add a lot of engagement but aren't required.
  5. Rotation: 1 → 2 → … → 8 → 1, about 10 minutes each. Station 2 (calculations) takes the longest; let fast groups start there.

Station 8

Directions

Equilibrium in Real Life

The fizzy soda. In a sealed bottle, carbon dioxide dissolved in the soda is in equilibrium with CO₂ gas in the space at the top: CO₂(dissolved) ⇌ CO₂(gas). The bottle is filled under high pressure, which keeps most of the CO₂ dissolved. When you open the cap, the pressure drops. The equilibrium shifts toward the gas, and bubbles stream out. A warm soda goes flat even faster, because gases dissolve less at higher temperatures.

Breathing on a mountain. In your blood, hemoglobin picks up oxygen: hemoglobin + O₂ ⇌ oxyhemoglobin. High in the mountains, each breath brings in less oxygen, so the equilibrium shifts left and your blood carries less oxygen. Climbers feel tired and dizzy. Over several weeks at high altitude, the body makes more hemoglobin, which shifts the equilibrium back to the right.

Feeding the world. Farmers need ammonia (NH₃) for fertilizer. Factories make it with N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ + heat. To get as much ammonia as possible, they squeeze the gases to very high pressure and remove ammonia as it forms. Both stresses push the equilibrium to the right.
