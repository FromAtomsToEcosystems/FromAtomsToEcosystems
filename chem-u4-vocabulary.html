The Mole and Counting by Weighing
MoleA counting unit for particles: 1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles.1 mol of water molecules
Avogadro's numberThe number of particles in one mole.6.02 × 10²³
Molar massThe mass of one mole of a substance, in g/mol.H₂O: 18.02 g/mol
Counting by weighingFinding how many objects you have by dividing the total mass by the mass of one.250 g of pennies ÷ 2.50 g = 100 pennies
ParticleOne atom (for an element) or one molecule (for a compound).One Fe atom; one CO₂ molecule
Dimensional Analysis
EqualityTwo amounts that are the same, written with an equals sign.1 dozen = 12 eggs
Conversion factorA fraction made from an equality. It equals 1, so it changes units but not the amount.12 eggs / 1 dozen
Dimensional analysisSolving problems by multiplying by conversion factors so units cancel.3 dozen × 12 eggs/1 dozen = 36 eggs
Bridge methodWriting the starting amount, then factors with the unit you have on the bottom so it cancels.36.04 g × 1 mol/18.02 g
Cancel (units)A unit on the top and bottom crosses out, leaving the unit you want.g on top and bottom cancel
The Mole Map
Mole mapA diagram showing that grams, particles, and liters of gas all connect through moles.grams ↔ moles ↔ particles
STPStandard temperature and pressure: 0 °C and 1 atmosphere.Conditions for 22.4 L/mol
Molar volumeAt STP, one mole of any gas takes up 22.4 L.2.00 mol O₂ = 44.8 L
Grams → molesDivide by the molar mass (grams on the bottom of the factor).88.02 g CO₂ ÷ 44.01 = 2.00 mol
Moles → particlesMultiply by Avogadro's number.0.500 mol × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³
Scientific notationA way to write very large or small numbers as a number times a power of 10.1.20 × 10²⁴
Multi-Step Conversions
Two-step conversionA conversion that goes into moles and then out of moles because neither end is moles.grams → moles → molecules
RouteThe plan of steps through the mole map.liters → moles → grams
Reasonable answerAn answer that makes sense when you estimate; samples you can hold have about 10²²–10²⁵ particles.2 g of water ≈ 10²² molecules, not 10⁴⁸
Stoichiometry
StoichiometryUsing a balanced equation to calculate amounts of reactants and products.How much CO₂ from 16 g CH₄?
CoefficientThe number in front of a formula in a balanced equation; it counts moles or particles.The 2 in 2H₂O
Mole ratioA conversion factor made from two coefficients in a balanced equation.2 mol H₂O / 1 mol O₂
Mole-to-moleChanging moles of one substance into moles of another with the mole ratio.3.00 mol O₂ → 6.00 mol H₂O
Mass-to-massgrams A → moles A → mole ratio → moles B → grams B.4.04 g H₂ → 36.04 g H₂O
Predicted (theoretical) amountThe amount of product stoichiometry says should form.8.40 g NaHCO₃ → 4.40 g CO₂
Actual amountThe amount of product measured in the lab.The scale shows 3.90 g lost
Percent of predictedActual ÷ predicted × 100.3.90 ÷ 4.40 × 100 = 89%
Balanced equationAn equation with the same number of each atom on both sides; needed for correct mole ratios.2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
