The Carbon Cycle

Every year, the CO₂ in the air goes up and down like a heartbeat, and every year it ends a little higher. Follow one carbon atom through air, plants, animals, oceans, and rocks to find out why.

Objective

I can explain how photosynthesis, cellular respiration, decomposition, eating, ocean exchange, fossil fuel formation, and burning move carbon among the atmosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere, and use this to explain the Keeling curve.

Standards

NGSS HS-LS2-5 Develop a model to illustrate the role of photosynthesis and cellular respiration in the cycling of carbon among the biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere.

Builds on Unit 2 Photosynthesis (in chloroplasts) and cellular respiration (in mitochondria) are the two processes that move carbon into and out of living things.
