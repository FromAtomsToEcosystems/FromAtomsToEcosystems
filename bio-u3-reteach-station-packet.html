Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 5 envelopes: label them 1, 2, 5, 7 (and a spare)
  • Scissors; card stock for Station 1 (optional)
  • 3 copies of the Codon Chart page (Stations 4, 6, 8)
  • Color printing for Stations 1 and 2 if possible

Station 1 setup: Build a DNA Ladder

Students build the complementary strand of DNA with shape-coded nucleotide pieces (A fits only T, C fits only G).

  1. Print the DNA Ladder Mat and the Station 1 cut-out page (color card stock is best). Cut out the 16 nucleotide pieces and put them in Envelope 1.
  2. Set out the mat, envelope, and direction page.
Tip: The base colors match the lessons: A red, T yellow, G green, C blue.

Station 2 setup: How DNA Copies Itself

Students order the steps of DNA replication and choose the semi-conservative model from three pictures.

  1. Print the Station 2 cut-out page. Cut out the 4 step cards and put them in Envelope 2.
  2. Set out the direction page (color helps: dark blue = old strand, light blue = new strand).
Tip: Model 3 is a good discussion: ask why a mixed-up copy would be harder to check for mistakes.

Station 3 setup: Transcription Practice

Self-checking flip cards: students transcribe 6 DNA template strands into mRNA, then flip to check.

  1. Print the 3 Station 3 flip-card pages. Cut each page down the middle to make 6 tall cards.
  2. Fold each card so the answer hides behind the question.
  3. Set out the cards and the direction page.
Tip: All 6 templates start with TAC, so every mRNA starts with AUG (the start codon). Ask students why that might be.

Station 1: Build a DNA Ladder

DNA is a double helix, like a twisted ladder. Each side of the ladder is a chain of nucleotides. A nucleotide has three parts: a phosphate, a sugar (deoxyribose), and a base. The sugars and phosphates make the sides. The bases make the rungs.

Bases always pair the same way (base pairing rule): A pairs with T, and C pairs with G. That is why the two strands are called complementary.

Directions:

  1. Open Envelope 1. Look at the template strand on the DNA Ladder Mat.
  2. For each base on the template, find the nucleotide piece that fits. The shapes only fit the correct partner.
  3. Read your new strand from top to bottom and record it. Answer the questions.
  4. Reset: put the pieces back in Envelope 1.

Station 4: Codon Chart Secret Messages

At the ribosome, mRNA is read three bases at a time. Each group of three is a codon. Each codon stands for one amino acid (or STOP). This is translation. Every protein starts with the start codon AUG (methionine, Met) and ends at a stop codon.

Each amino acid has a one-letter code, shown in ( ) on the codon chart. Translate each message, then write the one-letter codes to spell a secret word!

How to use the chart: find the 1st base on the left, the 2nd base on top, then the 3rd base inside the box. Example: CAU → row C, column A, line U → His (H).

Message 1: AUG GCU ACA CAU UAA
Message 2: AUG GCA GGU AUU UGC UAG
Message 3: AUG UAC ACU CAC UGA
Message 4: AUG GAG GAU GCA CUC UAA
Message 5: AUG AUC CGA GCG UGU UUA GAA UAG
Message 6: AUG AUA CUG AAG UCA CAU GCC AAA GAG UAA

Directions: Use the Codon Chart page at this station. Decode at least 4 of the 6 messages. Then answer the questions.

Station 6: Mutation Detectives

A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. When the DNA changes, the mRNA changes too, and the protein may change.

TypeWhat happensEffect on the protein
Substitution: silentone base swappedsame amino acid, no change
Substitution: missenseone base swappedone amino acid changes
Substitution: nonsenseone base swappedmakes a stop codon; protein is cut short
Insertion or deletion: frameshifta base added or removedevery codon after it shifts; many amino acids change

Directions:

  1. Look at the 8 mutation cards. Compare the mutated mRNA to the original. Use the codon chart at this station.
  2. Predict the type of mutation for each card. Then flip it open to check.
  3. Reset: fold every card closed.
Tip: line up the codons. Find the first place where they are different. Then ask: did one base swap, or did the grouping shift?

Station 8: Sickle Cell: A Real Mutation

Hemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Below are the first 9 codons of the mRNA for one part of hemoglobin (the beta chain) from two people.

Person 1: AUG GUG CAC CUG ACU CCU GAG GAG AAG
Person 2: AUG GUG CAC CUG ACU CCU GUG GAG AAG

Person 2 has sickle cell disease. Their hemoglobin sticks together into long fibers when oxygen is low. This bends the red blood cells into a curved, sickle shape. Sickle cells can get stuck in small blood vessels and cause pain.

Answer Key

Station 1: Build a DNA Ladder

1. A T G C G T A C. 2. Phosphate, sugar (deoxyribose), and a nitrogen base.

3. T = 30% (A pairs with T, so they are equal). A + T = 60%, so C + G = 40%, and G = 20%.

4. Each base can only pair with its partner, so one strand tells you exactly what the other strand must be. This lets DNA be copied correctly.

Station 2: How DNA Copies Itself

1. Helicase unzips; each strand is a template; DNA polymerase adds matching nucleotides; two molecules, each with one old and one new strand.

2. Model 2. "Semi" means half: each new DNA molecule keeps (conserves) one old strand and has one new strand.

3. Helicase: unzips the DNA by breaking hydrogen bonds between bases. DNA polymerase: adds new nucleotides that match the template strand.

Station 3: Transcription Practice

1. 1: AUG CCU AGU; 2: AUG GAA UUC; 3: AUG UCC GAA; 4: AUG AAA GGC; 5: AUG CGC AUU; 6: AUG UUG CCA.

2. Uracil (U). 3. DNA has to stay safe inside the nucleus. mRNA is a disposable copy that can leave through a nuclear pore and go to a ribosome.

Station 4: Codon Chart Secret Messages

1. 1: Met-Ala-Thr-His = MATH; 2: Met-Ala-Gly-Ile-Cys = MAGIC; 3: Met-Tyr-Thr-His = MYTH; 4: Met-Glu-Asp-Ala-Leu = MEDAL; 5: Met-Ile-Arg-Ala-Cys-Leu-Glu = MIRACLE; 6: Met-Ile-Leu-Lys-Ser-His-Ala-Lys-Glu = MILKSHAKE.

2. AUG UAA ... = Met then STOP. The protein would be just one amino acid long (a nonsense mutation).

3. tRNA. Each tRNA has an anticodon that pairs with the matching codon on the mRNA.

Station 5: From Gene to Protein

1. In order: C E A H F B G D.

2. Nucleus: C E A (the mRNA leaving through the pore can go on the edge). Cytoplasm: H F B G D.

3. mRNA. 4. Transcription copies a gene (DNA) into mRNA in the nucleus. Translation reads the mRNA codons at a ribosome and links amino acids into a protein.

Station 6: Mutation Detectives

1. 1: silent; 2: missense; 3: nonsense; 4: frameshift (insertion); 5: frameshift (deletion); 6: missense; 7: silent; 8: nonsense.

2. Adding or removing one base shifts the reading frame, so every codon after the mutation is read in the wrong groups of three. A substitution changes at most one codon.

Station 7: The Fat Cat Sentence

1. A: THE FAT RAT ATE THE RAT (makes sense, but different meaning). B: THE EFA TCA TAT ETH ERA T (nonsense after the change). C: THE ATC ATA TET HER AT (nonsense). D: THE FAT CAT ATE THE ROT (makes sense; one word changes).

2. B and C (insertion and deletion). Every "word" after the change was regrouped wrong, so the rest of the sentence became nonsense.

3. A missense substitution: one word (amino acid) changes, the rest stays the same. (Card A is also a missense model.)

Station 8: Sickle Cell: A Real Mutation

1. Codon 7 (counting AUG as 1): GAG → GUG.

2. Person 1: Glu (glutamic acid). Person 2: Val (valine).

3. A substitution, missense (one amino acid changes).

4. The new amino acid changes the shape and behavior of the hemoglobin protein. The proteins stick together, which bends the red blood cells into sickles that block blood vessels and carry less oxygen. A protein's shape decides its job.
