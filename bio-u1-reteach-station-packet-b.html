Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 4 envelopes: label them 1, 3, 4 (and a spare)
  • About 300 flat toothpicks, a cup with a lid, tape, and a timer (Station 8)
  • Scissors
  • Color printing for Stations 5 and 7 if possible
  • Real food labels (optional, Station 2)

Station 8: Toothpickase

In this model, your hand is an enzyme called "toothpickase." Toothpicks are the substrate. Breaking a toothpick in half is the reaction. The two halves are the products.

Directions: (one breaker, one timer, one counter; switch jobs each trial)

  1. Trial 1: Spread 20 toothpicks on the table. The breaker uses one hand only and breaks as many as possible in 20 seconds. Count the broken ones.
  2. Trial 2: Spread 60 toothpicks. Repeat. (More substrate.)
  3. Trial 3: Spread 60 toothpicks in a cup with a lid on. The breaker must take each one out first. (The substrate is harder to reach, like a cold or crowded reaction.)
  4. Trial 4: Tape the breaker's thumb to their first finger. Repeat with 60 toothpicks. (A "denatured" enzyme.)
  5. Record your data and answer the questions. Reset: put whole toothpicks back in the bag; throw broken ones away.
Each broken toothpick must be put in a pile before you pick up the next one, just like products leaving the active site.

Answer Key

Station 1: Macromolecule Word Match

1. Monomer: One small building block (One glucose); Polymer: A long chain of many monomers (Starch); Dehydration synthesis: Joins monomers by removing water (Your liver making glycogen); Hydrolysis: Breaks a polymer by adding water (Digesting bread); Carbohydrate: Sugars and starches for quick energy (Pasta); Lipid: Fats and oils that do not mix with water (Butter); Protein: A chain of amino acids that folds into a shape (An enzyme); Nucleic acid: Stores genetic information (DNA).

2. Dehydration synthesis and hydrolysis: one removes water to build, the other adds water to break apart.

Station 2: Reading Food Labels

1. Peanut butter (16 g fat). Protein: skim milk (8 g) is highest per serving (peanut butter 7 g).

2. All of it is sugar (lactose, milk sugar), a simple sugar.

3. Starch and fiber (complex carbohydrates, polysaccharides).

4. Granola bar or skim milk: both have 12 g of sugar per serving. Accept either with the sugar data.

Station 3: Which Macromolecule Am I?

1. Carbohydrate: Made of C, H, and O in about a 1 : 2 : 1 ratio, Cellulose in plant cell walls, Glycogen stored in muscles; Lipid: Does not mix with water, Phospholipids in the cell membrane, Long-term energy and insulation; Protein: Contains nitrogen in every amino acid, Hemoglobin carries oxygen, Antibodies fight germs; Nucleic acid: Built from nucleotides, RNA carries copies of genes, Has the bases A, T, C, and G.

2. Answers vary.

Station 7: Faster, Slower, or Stopped?

1. 1: Faster; 2: Stopped (denatured); 3: Slower; 4: Faster; 5: No change; 6: Slower or stopped; 7: Faster; 8: Much slower.

2. Cold slows molecules but does not change the enzyme's shape. Warming it back up restores its speed. High heat denatures it.

Station 8: Toothpickase

1. Data varies; trial 2 is usually higher than trial 1, trial 3 lower, trial 4 much lower.

2. Usually yes: more substrate means more "collisions" with the enzyme, until the enzyme works as fast as it can.

3. A denatured enzyme (changed shape); the rate drops a lot.

4. Enzymes are not used up; they can be used again and again.
