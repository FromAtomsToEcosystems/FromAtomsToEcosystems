Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 4 envelopes: label them 1, 4, 5 (and a spare)
  • Scissors; scrap paper for Punnett squares (Stations 3 and 7)
  • Calculators (Station 2)

Station 1: Genotype or Phenotype? Homozygous or Heterozygous?

A genotype is the pair of alleles (letters). A phenotype is the trait you can see. A genotype with two of the same letter is homozygous; two different letters is heterozygous.

Directions:

  1. Open Envelope 1. Lay out the 3 heading cards.
  2. Sort the 12 cards under the correct heading. Talk it over with your team before you place each one.
  3. Record your sort and answer the questions.
  4. Reset: put every card back in Envelope 1.

Station 6: Queen Victoria's Hemophilia

A real family history.

Queen Victoria of England (1819 to 1901) was a carrier of hemophilia, an X-linked recessive disease where blood does not clot well. Her husband Albert did not have it.

ChildSexWhat history tells us
Leopoldsonhad hemophilia
Alicedaughterdid not have it, but some of her sons did
Beatricedaughterdid not have it, but some of her sons did
Edward VIIsondid not have it

Through her daughters, the allele spread to the royal families of Spain and Russia. Use XH (normal clotting) and Xh (hemophilia).

Directions: Study the data with your team and answer the Station 6 questions on your recording sheet.

Answer Key

Station 1: Genotype or Phenotype? Homozygous or Heterozygous?

1. Phenotype: Purple flowers, Type AB blood, Wrinkled seeds, Red-green color blind; Genotype: homozygous: PP, tt, IAIA, RR (red snapdragon); Genotype: heterozygous: Pp, IAi, RW (pink snapdragon), XBXb.

2. No. A purple flower could be PP or Pp, because P is dominant.

Station 2: Mendel's Real Pea Data

1. Seed color 6022 ÷ 2001 ≈ 3.01; flower color 705 ÷ 224 ≈ 3.15; height 787 ÷ 277 ≈ 2.84.

2. 3 : 1 (dominant : recessive).

3. Each offspring is a random chance event; real counts vary a little, but with large numbers they get close to 3 : 1.

Station 3: Work Backward: Find the Parents

1. 1: BB × bb; 2: Tt × Tt; 3: Rr × rr; 4: RR × RW; 5: IAi × IBi; 6: XBXb × XBY.

2. Type O is ii, so each parent must have given an i allele.

Station 4: How a Karyotype Is Made

1. B E C A F D (Take a sample of cells, often from blood; Grow the cells and let them start to divide; Stop the cells when the chromosomes are most coiled and easy to see; Stain the chromosomes so they show stripes (bands); Take a picture through a microscope; Arrange the chromosomes in pairs from largest to smallest).

2. That is when chromosomes are tightly coiled and copied, so they are easiest to see.

3. Can show: sex, extra or missing chromosomes (like trisomy 21), large pieces missing. Cannot show: single-gene alleles like blood type.

Station 5: Name the Inheritance Pattern

1. Complete dominance: A Tt pea plant is just as tall as a TT plant, A Bb mouse has black fur, like BB; Incomplete dominance: Red × white snapdragons make pink flowers, Curly × straight hair parents have a wavy-haired child (in a simple model); Codominance: A roan cow has both red and white hairs, Type AB blood shows both A and B markers; Sex-linked: Hemophilia is far more common in males, A carrier mother passes red-green color blindness to half her sons.

2. Incomplete: a blend (in between). Codominance: both traits show fully, side by side.

Station 6: Queen Victoria's Hemophilia

1. Victoria XHXh; Albert XHY; Leopold XhY; Alice XHXh (carrier).

2. XHXH, XHXh, XHY, XhY: 50% of sons.

3. Every daughter got Albert's XH, so at most they were carriers. 4. He got Victoria's XH (a 50% chance) and Albert's Y.

Station 7: Quick Crosses

1. 1: 50% pink; 2: 50% roan; 3: A or B (50% each); 4: 25%; 5: 100% of sons; 6: 1/2 (TT or tt).

2. Answers vary.

Station 8: Draw the Pedigree

1. Generation I: Maria (circle) and José (square), unshaded. II: Ana (shaded circle), Luis (square), Rosa (circle); Luis married to Grace (shaded circle). III: Sam (shaded square) under Luis and Grace.

2. Maria Aa, José Aa, Luis Aa (he had a child with albinism).

3. Two unaffected parents (Maria and José) had an affected child.
