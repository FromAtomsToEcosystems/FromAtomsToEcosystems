TermDefinitionExample
Counting Atoms
Chemical formulaSymbols and numbers that show which elements are in a substance and how many atoms of each.H₂O, CO₂
SubscriptA small number after a symbol that counts the atoms of the element right before it.The 2 in H₂O
CoefficientA big number in front of a formula that counts how many molecules (or units) there are.The 3 in 3H₂O
Parentheses (in formulas)Group atoms; a subscript outside multiplies everything inside.Ca(OH)₂ has 2 O and 2 H
MoleculeA group of atoms bonded together; the smallest unit of a covalent substance.One H₂O
Particle diagramA drawing where each ball is an atom and touching atoms form one molecule.2H₂O drawn as two clusters
Chemical Reactions
Chemical reactionA process where atoms rearrange to form new substances.Iron rusting
Chemical equationA recipe for a reaction written with formulas: reactants → products.2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
ReactantsThe starting substances, written on the left of the arrow.H₂ and O₂
ProductsThe new substances made, written on the right of the arrow.H₂O
Evidence of a reactionSigns that new substances formed: gas, color change, solid forming, heat or light.Bubbles in vinegar
PrecipitateA solid that forms when two solutions are mixed.White AgCl
HydrocarbonA compound made of only carbon and hydrogen; a common fuel.CH₄, C₃H₈
Types of Reactions
SynthesisTwo or more reactants combine into one product: A + B → AB.2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
DecompositionOne reactant breaks into two or more products: AB → A + B.2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
Single replacementA lone element replaces another element in a compound: A + BC → AC + B.Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
Double replacementTwo compounds trade partners: AB + CD → AD + CB.AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
CombustionA fuel burns in oxygen; a hydrocarbon makes CO₂ and H₂O.CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
Balancing and Conservation
Law of conservation of massAtoms are not created or destroyed in a reaction, so mass stays the same.Sealed bag: 85.0 g before and after
Balanced equationAn equation with the same number of each kind of atom on both sides.N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
Open systemA container where matter can enter or leave.An open cup
Closed systemA container where nothing can enter or leave.A sealed bag
Polyatomic ionA group of atoms that acts as one unit with a charge.NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, OH⁻
Lowest whole-number ratioCoefficients reduced as far as possible.2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, not 4H₂ + 2O₂
Molar Mass
MoleA counting unit for particles: 1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles.1 mol of water molecules
Avogadro's numberThe number of particles in one mole.6.02 × 10²³
Atomic massThe average mass of one atom, shown as the decimal number on the periodic table.Carbon: 12.01
Molar massThe mass in grams of one mole of a substance (g/mol).H₂O: 18.02 g/mol
Grams → molesDivide the mass by the molar mass.36.04 g ÷ 18.02 g/mol = 2.00 mol
Moles → gramsMultiply the moles by the molar mass.2.00 mol × 44.01 g/mol = 88.02 g
