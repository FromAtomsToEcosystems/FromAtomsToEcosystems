Unit 5 Stations B

Teacher guide: Unit 5 Stations B (heating and cooling curves and gas pressure)

Use after Day 8 (boiling under low pressure lab). 8 stations, one per group of 4, about 10 minutes each. Students carry the paper reflection sheet or use chem-u5-stations-b-reflection.html on a Chromebook (it saves and sends scores to the Google Sheet).

  1. Print the station pages (one per table) and the card pages.
  2. Fold-over cards (Station 2): cut out each card, fold on the dashed line so the answer hides behind the question, and tape the fold.
  3. Cut cards (Station 4): cut out the 5 description cards and put them in an envelope.
  4. Optional hands-on add-ons: a sealed bag of chips at Station 5, and a plastic syringe with the tip capped at Station 8 (push the plunger to feel the pressure rise).
  5. Rotation: 1 → 2 → … → 8 → 1, about 10 minutes each. Stations 3 and 6 (calculations) take the longest.

The answer key is behind the teacher code.

Station 5

Chip Bag on a Plane

Reading · about 10 min

Directions

  1. Read the article with your group.
  2. Answer the reflection questions.

The Puffy Chip Bag

A bag of chips is sealed at a factory near sea level, where the air pressure is about 1.00 atm. Inside the bag, air particles hit the inside walls; outside, air particles hit the outside walls. The pushes balance, so the bag looks normal.

On an airplane, the cabin is kept at only about 0.75 atm, about the air pressure on top of a 2,400 m mountain. Now fewer air particles hit the outside of the bag each second, but the air inside still pushes at about 1.00 atm. The bag puffs up like a pillow. When the plane lands, the outside pressure rises again and the bag flattens back down.

Gas pressure comes from particles colliding with surfaces. More particles, or faster particles, mean more collisions and more pressure.
