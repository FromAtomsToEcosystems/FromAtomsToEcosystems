Unit 6 Stations B

Teacher guide: Unit 6 Stations B (solubility, molarity, dilutions, and acids and bases)

Use after Day 12 (red cabbage pH lab). 8 stations, one per group of 4, about 10 minutes each. Students carry the paper reflection sheet or use chem-u6-stations-b-reflection.html on a Chromebook (it saves and sends scores to the Google Sheet).

  1. Print the station pages (one per table) and the card pages.
  2. Fold-over cards (Station 2): cut out each card, fold on the dashed line so the answer hides behind the question, and tape the fold. Students predict, then lift the flap.
  3. Cut cards (Stations 4, 6, and 7): cut out and put each set in a labeled envelope. Remind groups to put the cards back before rotating.
  4. Optional hands-on add-ons: a cup of water and sugar or salt with a spoon at Station 1 (try to dissolve "too much"), and pH paper with a few safe household liquids (vinegar, soapy water, water) at Station 7.
  5. Rotation: 1 → 2 → … → 8 → 1, about 10 minutes each. Stations 3 and 5 (calculations) take the longest; let fast groups start there.

Reading · about 10 min

Directions

  1. Read the article with your group.

Neutralization in Real Life

Heartburn. Your stomach makes hydrochloric acid (pH about 1.5) to digest food. When some splashes into your esophagus, it burns. Antacid tablets contain bases such as calcium carbonate or magnesium hydroxide. The base neutralizes some of the acid, making a salt and water, and the pH rises so the burning stops.

Acidic lakes. Acid rain can lower a lake's pH to 5 or below, and many fish and their eggs can't survive. Some towns drop crushed limestone (calcium carbonate, a base) into the lake from boats or helicopters. It neutralizes the acid and raises the pH back toward 7.

Gardens. Some crops grow poorly in acidic soil. Farmers spread garden lime (another base) on fields to raise the soil's pH. Blueberries are different: they like acidic soil, so gardeners sometimes add sulfur to lower the pH.

Bee stings. A bee sting is slightly acidic. A paste of baking soda (a weak base) and water can neutralize some of the acid and ease the sting.
