States of Matter and Kinetic Energy
SolidParticles packed in fixed positions that vibrate; definite shape and volume.An ice cube
LiquidParticles close together that slide past each other; definite volume, takes the container's shape.Water in a glass
GasParticles far apart and moving fast; fills its container and can be compressed.Steam
Kinetic energyEnergy of motion. Faster particles have more kinetic energy.Hot particles move faster
TemperatureA measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a sample.300 K
Absolute zeroThe lowest possible temperature: 0 K = −273 °C.0 K
Kelvin scaleA temperature scale that starts at absolute zero. K = °C + 273.25 °C = 298 K
Vapor Pressure and Boiling
EvaporationFast particles escaping from the surface of a liquid to become a gas.A puddle drying
Vapor pressureThe pressure of a vapor above its liquid in a sealed container at equilibrium.Water at 25 °C: 24 mmHg
EquilibriumA balance where two opposite processes happen at the same rate.Escape rate = return rate
Boiling pointThe temperature where a liquid's vapor pressure equals the surrounding pressure.Water in Denver: 95 °C
Normal boiling pointThe boiling point at 1 atm (760 mmHg).Water: 100 °C
VolatileEvaporates easily; has a high vapor pressure.Diethyl ether
Phase Diagrams
Phase changeA change from one state to another.Melting, boiling
SublimationSolid → gas without becoming a liquid.Dry ice at 1 atm
DepositionGas → solid without becoming a liquid.Frost on a window
Phase diagramA graph of pressure vs. temperature showing the phase at any conditions.Water's phase diagram
Triple pointThe temperature and pressure where solid, liquid, and gas all exist together.CO₂: −57 °C, 5.1 atm
Critical pointThe end of the liquid–gas line; above it, liquid and gas can't be told apart.Water: 374 °C, 218 atm
Heating Curves
Heating curveA graph of temperature as a substance is heated steadily.Ice → steam
PlateauA flat part of a heating or cooling curve where a phase change happens at constant temperature.Water boiling at 100 °C
Potential energyEnergy stored in attractions between particles; it changes during phase changes.Increases while ice melts
Heat of fusionEnergy needed to melt 1 g of a solid at its melting point.Ice: 334 J/g
Heat of vaporizationEnergy needed to boil 1 g of a liquid at its boiling point.Water: 2,260 J/g
Cooling curveA heating curve in reverse; condensation and freezing release energy.Steam cooling to ice
Gas Pressure
Gas pressureThe push of gas particles hitting the walls of their container.Air in a tire
BarometerAn instrument that measures air pressure.Mercury barometer: 760 mm at sea level
Atmosphere (atm)A pressure unit equal to normal air pressure at sea level.1 atm = 760 mmHg
mmHg / torrMillimeters of mercury; 1 torr = 1 mmHg.760 torr = 1 atm
Kilopascal (kPa)The metric pressure unit.1 atm = 101.3 kPa
