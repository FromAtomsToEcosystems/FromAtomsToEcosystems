Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 4 envelopes: label them 1, 2, 3, 8
  • Scissors
  • Color printing for Stations 4, 5, and 7 if possible
  • Optional demos: raisins in water, salted strawberries, wilted celery in water

Answer Key

Station 1: Energy Word Match

1. ATP: The energy currency of the cell (A muscle cell spends it to contract); Photosynthesis: Makes glucose using light (A lettuce leaf in the sun); Cellular respiration: Breaks down glucose to make ATP (Your heart cells, all day); Fermentation: Makes 2 ATP without oxygen (Yeast in bread dough); Chloroplast: Organelle of photosynthesis (Green parts of a spinach cell); Mitochondrion: Organelle of respiration (Hundreds in each muscle cell); Reactant: An input to a reaction (CO₂ for photosynthesis); Product: An output of a reaction (Oxygen from photosynthesis).

2. Chloroplast (photosynthesis) and mitochondrion (cellular respiration).

Station 2: In or Out?

1. Photosynthesis input: Carbon dioxide, Water, Light energy; Photosynthesis output: Glucose, Oxygen; Respiration input: Glucose (as food), Oxygen (breathed in); Respiration output: Carbon dioxide (breathed out), Water (as vapor), ATP.

2. Glucose, oxygen, CO₂, and water. The products of one process are the reactants of the other: matter cycles between them.

Station 3: Follow the Light

1. B C F E D A (Chlorophyll in the thylakoids absorbs red and blue light; Light energy splits water molecules; Oxygen gas is released from the leaf; The light reactions make ATP; CO₂ enters the leaf and the stroma; The Calvin cycle uses ATP to build glucose).

2. Thylakoids: absorbing light, splitting water, releasing oxygen, making ATP. Stroma: CO₂ in, the Calvin cycle builds glucose.

3. From splitting water.

Station 4: Which Colors Do Leaves Use?

1. Violet/blue and red/orange. 2. Green; it is reflected, which is why leaves look green.

3. It grows poorly: green light is mostly reflected, not absorbed, so little light energy powers photosynthesis.

Station 5: Where Does It Happen?

1. 1: Cytoplasm; 2: Mitochondrial matrix; 3: Inner mitochondrial membrane; 4: Thylakoids; 5: Stroma; 6: Cytoplasm of muscle cells.

2. Fermentation also happens in the cytoplasm.

Station 6: Yeast With and Without Oxygen

1. A aerobic; B fermentation (ethanol smell). 2. Aerobic respiration breaks glucose all the way down (Krebs cycle releases more CO₂) and makes much more ATP, so yeast grow and work faster.

3. A control with no glucose: it shows yeast need sugar. 4. Aerobic about 36; fermentation 2.

Station 7: Which Way Will Water Go?

1. 1: Into the raisins: hypotonic; 2: Out of the cells: hypertonic; 3: Both ways equally: isotonic; 4: Into the cells: hypotonic; 5: Out of the cells: hypertonic; 6: Both ways equally: isotonic.

2. Salty soil is hypertonic, so water leaves the root cells by osmosis and the plant wilts.

Station 8: Passive or Active?

1. Passive: Oxygen enters a red blood cell, CO₂ leaves a muscle cell, Water enters a root hair, Glucose moves through a carrier protein, high to low; Active: Sodium-potassium pump, An amoeba engulfs food (endocytosis), A nerve cell releases chemicals in vesicles (exocytosis), A plant root takes in nitrate that is already more concentrated inside.

2. It moves high to low, down the gradient, so it needs no ATP. The protein is only a doorway.
