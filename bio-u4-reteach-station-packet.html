Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 2 envelopes: label them 1 and 5
  • 2 coins (or 2 cups with B and b cards) for Station 2
  • Scissors
  • Color printing for Stations 4, 5, and 6
  • Magnifying glass (optional, Station 8)

Station 2 setup: Coin Toss Babies

A hands-on probability station: students flip two coins (two Bb parents) to make 20 "babies" and compare results to the Punnett square.

  1. Set out 2 coins (or 2 cups, each with a B card and a b card).
  2. Set out the direction page.
Tip: Pool the class data on the board at the end. With more trials, results get closer to 25% : 50% : 25%.

  1. Print the 3 Station 3 flip-card pages. Cut each page down the middle and fold each card on the dashed line.
  2. Set out the cards, the direction page, and scrap paper.
Tip: Students often confuse ratio and percent. Have them write both: 1 out of 4 = 25%.

Station 1: Genetics Vocabulary Match

Genetics has a lot of new words. Many of them come in pairs (dominant and recessive, homozygous and heterozygous, genotype and phenotype). In this station, your team matches each term to its definition and an example.

All the examples use one trait: fur color in a mouse. B = black fur (dominant) and b = brown fur (recessive).

Directions:

  1. Open Envelope 1. Lay out the 8 term cards in a column.
  2. Place the matching definition card and example card next to each term.
  3. Record your matches. Then answer the questions. Reset: put all cards back in Envelope 1.

Station 2: Coin Toss Babies

Each parent has two alleles for a gene but passes only one to each child. Which one is a matter of chance, like flipping a coin. A Punnett square predicts the probability of each genotype. Do real results match the prediction?

Both parents are heterozygous for fur color: Bb × Bb. Each coin is one parent.

Directions:

  1. Fill in the Punnett square on your recording sheet for Bb × Bb. Write the predicted percent of BB, Bb, and bb.
  2. Two partners each flip one coin at the same time. Heads = B, tails = b. Together the two coins are one baby's genotype.
  3. Make 20 babies. Tally each genotype.
  4. Compare your results to the prediction. Then answer the questions.
If you do not have coins: put a B card and a b card in each of two cups, and draw one from each cup without looking.

Station 3: Punnett Square Practice

A Punnett square shows all the possible ways alleles from two parents can combine.

  1. Write one parent's two alleles across the top and the other parent's two alleles down the side.
  2. Fill each box with one letter from the top and one from the side.
  3. Each box is a 1 in 4 (25%) chance.
Example: Aa × Aa gives AA, Aa, Aa, aa.
Genotype ratio 1 : 2 : 1.
If A is dominant, phenotype ratio 3 : 1.

Directions:

  1. Take a card. On your recording sheet, set up and fill in the Punnett square for the cross.
  2. Answer the card's question. Then flip it open to check.
  3. Do all 6 cards. Reset: fold every card closed.

Incomplete dominancea blend of the two traits (in between)red + white paint = pink
Codominanceboth traits show fully, side by side"co" = together, like co-workers

For these traits we use two capital letters, such as R (red) and W (white), because neither one is recessive.

Directions:

  2. For each card, predict what the offspring will look like and whether it is incomplete dominance or codominance.
  3. Flip each card open to check. Reset: fold every card closed.

Station 6: Sex-Linked Traits

Females have two X chromosomes (XX). Males have one X and one Y (XY). Genes on the X chromosome are sex-linked. A male has only one X, so if it carries a recessive allele, he will show the trait. A female needs two copies.

Red-green color blindness is a recessive, X-linked trait. We write XB (normal vision) and Xb (color blind). About 8% of males of European descent have it, but less than 1% of females.

An example color dot pattern like those used to check color vision. People with red-green color blindness may have trouble seeing the number. This is only a demonstration, not a medical test.

Directions: Use the Punnett squares on your recording sheet to answer the questions. Put the X and Y chromosomes with their alleles on the outside of each square.

Example: a carrier female is XBXb. She has normal vision but can pass Xb to her children.

Station 7: Pedigree Detective

A pedigree is a family tree that shows who has a trait. Squares are males and circles are females. A shaded shape has the trait. A horizontal line between a male and a female is a couple; a vertical line leads down to their children. Roman numerals are generations.

Clues for solving pedigrees:

  • Two parents without the trait have a child with it → the trait is recessive (both parents are carriers).
  • The trait shows in every generation, and every affected child has an affected parent → it is probably dominant.
  • Mostly males are affected, often passed through unaffected mothers → probably sex-linked recessive.

Directions: Study the three pedigrees on the Pedigree page. For each one, decide the pattern and answer the questions.

Station 8: Karyotype Check

A karyotype is a picture of all of a person's chromosomes, lined up in pairs from largest to smallest. Most people have 46 chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes (pairs 1 to 22) and 1 pair of sex chromosomes (pair 23: XX = female, XY = male).

Sometimes a mistake during meiosis (called nondisjunction) gives a person an extra or missing chromosome:

ConditionWhat the karyotype shows
Down syndromethree copies of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21), 47 total
Klinefelter syndromeXXY in pair 23, 47 total
Turner syndromeonly one X in pair 23, 45 total

Directions:

  1. Study Karyotypes A, B, and C on the next pages. Count carefully, especially pairs 21, 22, and 23.
  2. For each one, record the sex, the total number of chromosomes, and any condition.
