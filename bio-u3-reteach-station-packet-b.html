Teacher Guide

There are 8 stations. Groups of 3 or 4 students rotate through them. Each station has a direction page and a few questions on the Station Recording Sheet. Students carry this one sheet from station to station. Most stations take about 15 to 20 minutes, so all 8 stations take about 2 to 3 class periods (in a 90-minute period, about 4 stations). You can also pick only the stations that match what your students missed on the exit tickets.

Some stations need no prep beyond printing the direction page. Others need cut-out cards in envelopes. Every station ends with a Reset step so the next group finds it ready.

Required materials

  • 5 envelopes: label them 1, 2, 3, 6, 7
  • Scissors
  • 3 copies of the Codon Chart page (Stations 5, 6, and 8)

Station 8: Cystic Fibrosis: A Real Mutation

Compare the two DNA sequences from a real gene.

Typical gene: ... ATC ATC TTT GGT GTT ...
Person with CF: ... ATC ATT GGT GTT ...
(DNA coding strand, codons 506 to 510 of the CFTR gene. The mRNA is the same with U for T.)

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is caused by mutations in the CFTR gene. The CFTR protein is a channel that moves chloride ions out of cells. The most common CF mutation removes 3 bases. Without a working channel, thick mucus builds up in the lungs.

Directions: Study the data with your team and answer the Station 8 questions on your recording sheet.

Answer Key

Station 1: DNA and Protein Words

1. Nucleotide: Phosphate + sugar + base (One A on a DNA strand); Replication: Copying DNA before a cell divides (Helicase unzips the helix); Transcription: DNA to mRNA in the nucleus (TAC becomes AUG); Translation: mRNA to protein at a ribosome (AUG becomes Met); Codon: Three mRNA bases for one amino acid (GGC); Anticodon: Three tRNA bases that pair with a codon (CCG on a tRNA); Mutation: A change in the DNA sequence (Sickle cell); Gene: A section of DNA that codes for a protein (The insulin gene).

2. Replication and transcription.

Station 2: DNA, RNA, or Both?

1. DNA only: Double-stranded helix, Has the base thymine (T), Sugar is deoxyribose, Stays in the nucleus; RNA only: Single-stranded, Has the base uracil (U), Sugar is ribose, Can leave the nucleus; Both: Made of nucleotides, Has the bases A, C, and G, Is a nucleic acid, Carries genetic information.

2. DNA stays protected in the nucleus; disposable mRNA copies carry the message to ribosomes.

Station 3: Copy That DNA

1. B C F E D A (Helicase unzips the DNA by breaking the bonds between base pairs; The two strands separate, like a zipper opening; Each old strand becomes a template; DNA polymerase adds free nucleotides that match: A with T, C with G; Two identical DNA molecules are made; Each new molecule has one old strand and one new strand).

2. Helicase unzips; DNA polymerase adds matching nucleotides.

3. Each new DNA keeps (conserves) one old strand: "semi" means half.

Station 4: Chargaff's Rule

1. A and T; G and C. 2. In the double helix, A always pairs with T and G with C, so their amounts are equal.

3. G = 18%; A + T = 64%, so A = 32% and T = 32%.

Station 5: From DNA to Amino Acid

1. 1: mRNA AUG → Met (start); 2: mRNA UUU → Phe; 3: mRNA CCG → Pro; 4: mRNA UGA → STOP; 5: mRNA GAA → Glu; 6: mRNA GUA → Val.

2. ACT gives UGA, a stop codon: no tRNA matches, so the chain is released.

Station 6: What Kind of Mutation?

1. Silent: AUG CCC GAA UGG UAA, AUG CCA GAA UGG UAA; Missense: AUG CCU GUA UGG UAA, AUG UCU GAA UGG UAA; Nonsense: AUG CCU UAA UGG UAA, AUG CCU GAA UGA UAA; Frameshift: AUG CCU GGA AUG GUA A, AUG CUG AAU GGU AA.

2. Frameshift: every codon after the change is read in the wrong groups of three.

Station 7: Nucleus or Cytoplasm?

1. Nucleus: DNA is unzipped at one gene, RNA polymerase builds mRNA, DNA replication, Transcription; Cytoplasm (at a ribosome): mRNA attaches to a ribosome, tRNA anticodons pair with codons, Amino acids are joined by peptide bonds, Translation.

2. mRNA leaves through a nuclear pore.

Station 8: Cystic Fibrosis: A Real Mutation

1. 3 bases (CTT). 2. Typical: Ile Ile Phe Gly Val. CF: Ile Ile Gly Val. Phenylalanine (Phe) is missing (called delta F508).

3. Removing exactly 3 bases removes one whole codon, so the codons after it are still read in the right groups of three.

4. The protein folds into the wrong shape, so the chloride channel does not work; shape decides function.
